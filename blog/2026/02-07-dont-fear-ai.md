## *It's easy to see Artificial Intelligence as something brand new. That can be overwhelming, especially if you're not well versed with computers but, think of it like this: Just like when smartphone technology was invented, when high speed internet, even when the original computer was invented, what's feels so 'new' about a technology is the speed in which it moves.*

A computer was fascinating because it did what a calculator does but, many times, quicker, and with less human input. That's all. It is a fancy automated calculator. As technology has advanced, all that's happening, is that they are capable of doing more math, at a higher rate. You might have heard of "1's and Zero's" being computer language. It still is. You line up enough of them in particular order, they make up anything you've ever looked at on a screen.

'AI' is the same technology. What makes it unique is that the hardware driving it is going faster than ever. Hence all the 'data center,' 'hyperscaler' you see in the news as of late. 

This is not to disparage the trend. It truly is very fascinating. I am already leveraging it to enhance some of the knowledge and experience I didn't have, to build, learn, and create like never before. However, it's not a human brain. The 'AI' is not alive, all of a sudden. 

In fact, we've had 'AI' for quite some time. How do you think the self-playing piano worked? How did a 'Choose Your Own Adventure' book work? How did Oregon Trail decide if you won the game or not? 

It's a large thread of 'if else' and 'if and' math, coded to language that humans understand that makes it interesting. AI takes input from LLM (Large Language Models), the internet, and from you, and it runs it through these calculations (at remarkable speed, now) and operates much the same way as the old floppy disk games. 

## Change
Listen, it's going to change things. The 'logic' is going to revolutionize what is possible but, it's nothing to be intimidated by. If anything, if you didn't know anything about computers and coding, the playing field has just been leveled in your advantage. 

I've never understood how to code, completely. Yet, because I am capable of having a conversation, expressing my ideas, and yes, having some minimal experience with coding, I was able to leverage an AI assistant to build my own blog. As it progresses, it will become even more user friendly.

It's okay to be concerned about the hype. It's legitimately exciting but, it's not quite living up to the dream, just yet. However, I believe it will, and relatively quickly. 

So, instead of worrying about all of the bad potential, start familiarizing yourself with the technology. Would you want to be looking for a job, today in 2026, if you didn't know how to use a smartphone, or a calculator? 

Even better, ask your favorite AI how to use it. It can help you with that, too.

## How to Prepare
When I discovered AI, I didn't know anything beyond how quickly it could respond. I was naturally impressed by how quickly it could carry on one side of a conversation nearly immediately. It was like it was carrying on without me, how fast it would create logic out of thin air, and mostly on topic.

It wasn't until I began asking it questions about how to use it efficiently that I began to see a clearer picture. If you have enough computer skills to be reading, and understanding most of this, you might also be interested in working with artificial intelligence in the near future. 

I've been 'tinkering' with computers for years. I had my first desktop in my bedroom in 2002. I've taken it apart to install drives, and somehow managed to put it back together. Sometimes, I'm not sure how I did but, that Dell PC served me well for a long time. While I admit to not having much coding skill, I understand systems, and organization. What is an operating system but a filing cabinet running on electricity?

The point is, I know enough. What I don't know, I can figure out by searching the web. A lot of what I know about software has been trial and error, collecting data, analyzing it, and making adjustments. It's how I learned to master social media (you know, before it was gamified and rigged.) It's how I taught myself sound engineering and music production. It's why I can troubleshoot my parent's smart tv's and bluetooth headphones.

I'm a firm believer in "sometimes, you gotta get it wrong a whole bunch of times before you get it right." Failure is the best teacher. Everytime I thought I ruined my computer, I figured out a new way to fly, so to speak.

I say that to say this: if you want to utilize this new technology, there a few (more) techniques that I've picked up in recent weeks that you should definitely look into.

## Prompt Engineering
Don't just throw a random idea at a chat and see what it does. There's actually more to it than that. You can craft an entire identity for your "agent," or "assistant," or whatever you want to call her, or it, or whatever. My concern was token efficiency but, it goes for managing your time, too.  AI is like the know-it-all preppy kid who doesn't have much real life experience. More often than not, I find myself thinking: "Yeah. You're right but, it still doesn't work for this particular instance." 

[In a recent tweet, I remarked about the intelligence of AI being impressive but, it still needs a babysitter](https://x.com/JoshWritesWell/status/2023596357114843539?s=20). Imagine it as your gifted young child. A "Young Sheldon" type, if you will. It really does know everything but, it's lack of "experience" is glaring. So, instead of hopping into a chat with it, put your ideas together into a note. Check it a few times to make sure your own logic is clear because, it's not great at reading inference. You have to communicate in literal terms.

You can also define "who you're talking to." For instance, early on I was experimenting with creating custom profiles for specific fields of expertise. I gave the AI an identity before doing anything else. "You are an old friend, who happens to be a world class chef. You are incredibly skilled but, since we're old friends, you tolerate my ignorance, and are happy to entertain my random questions about food and cooking." I also tried to give her a flirty southern belle vibe but, the prudes are already handcuffing certain technologies, if you know what I mean. The point is. Give it a little narrative and you'll be surprised what it can reference. 

No short chats, at least until you've shaped the profile for your workflow. This is not only good for you but, it helps cut down on the energy usage. More on token efficiency once I get better data to analyze on the environmental front.

It also helps if you already know a little about what you're trying to get your assistant to do. I know a small amount of coding logic so, in the hundreds of times that my agent has messed up my website, I was able to troubleshoot well enough to explain the problem back to her. As far as I know, this page is still readable but, if it goes down again, you'll know why. *gestures at my free tiered AI bot*

## Which AI Should You Use?
Anyway, I won't even begin to claim expertise on this, yet. As an environmental science student, resource efficiency is a high priority so, I don't use the most advanced models. I am perfectly content utilizing the more efficient protocols, even if they lack some of the bells and whistles. Before you say it, it's not just because it's still free. I don't mind paying for a legitimate service but, I don't know for a fact that these "premium" models are going to fare any better with my prospective work load. When they're free platforms start impressing me, I'll consider paying for one. Until then, save yourself the money, and energy, and use the basic tiers. Aside from some of the logic issues I've already complained about, this one is still quite remarkable.

[I'm moving away from using names](index.html?page=jots) because, I don't want my frustrations to become a slippery slope in tearing down an entire company over it. I still entertain the possibility that these "mistakes" are not intentional, [however suspicious of the entire internet industry I am](https://x.com/JoshWritesWell/status/2022151381818429608?s=20_).

That's to say that, I'm not openly endorsing any specific model, just yet.

## None of them have the absolute power to replace you at work.
In reality, when you are replaced by AI, it ought to be by the one you designed to your exact specifications. So, look into prompt engineering and see where that rabbit hole takes you. Come back and share the knowledge if you find anything remarkable.

Good luck.
